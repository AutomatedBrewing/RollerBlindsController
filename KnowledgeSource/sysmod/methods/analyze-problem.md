Method 4.5 in SYSMOD. Sometimes the initial problem statement does not represent the real problem—a profound analysis is required to identify the true challenge. Design Thinking and problem-solving frameworks support this method.

# Purpose

Find out the real problem that should be resolved by the system. Sometimes it is not the initial problem statement, and the real problem must be identified by a profound analysis.

# Description

An illustrative example of the importance of finding the real problem is the quote attributed to Henry Ford: "If I had asked my customers what they wanted, they would have said a faster horse." The real problem to be solved and the real needs to be satisfied require a profound analysis.

Design Thinking is an approach to solve problems creatively with a particular focus on problem analysis. Part of the Design Thinking process is the redefinition of the initial problem statement to find the real challenge that satisfies the needs of the stakeholders. Design Thinking asks what the users need, not just what they want.

Another approach is the problem-solving process described by Reinhard Haberfellner et al., based on five steps of problem-solving by John Dews (1910). The process is logical rather than strictly sequential, with many feedback cycles.

Two simple workshop tools to explore the problem space are the 6M and Five Whys methods.
