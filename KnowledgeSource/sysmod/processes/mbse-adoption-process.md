The SYSMOD MBSE Adoption Process (SMAP) is the process for adopting MBSE in an organization or project. It is unique in that SYSMOD uses itself to define the MBSE Methodology: the system of interest is the MBSE Methodology rather than an engineering product.

# Purpose

SMAP guides organizations through the selection, tailoring, and deployment of MBSE. It provides a structured process to avoid common pitfalls such as resistance, poor tool selection, and insufficient training.

# Description

SMAP uses SYSMOD itself to elaborate the needs for a methodology and to derive the appropriate methods. The MBSE Methodology is treated as a system to be engineered: requirements are gathered from stakeholders (the engineering teams), the "product" is the customized methodology, and the deployment corresponds to the Architecture and Infrastructure processes.

Chapter 10 of the SYSMOD book provides additional details on the MBSE Adoption Process, including:

- Resistance and Anti-Patterns (common obstacles to MBSE adoption)
- Selection of the MBSE Methodology
- Selection of the Modeling Languages
- Selection of the Modeling Tools

The process runs parallel to the infrastructure process and should be executed before starting any engineering project with SYSMOD.
