Tool 11.12 in SYSMOD. The Five Whys method is the basis of Toyota's scientific approach, based on the idea that "the root cause of any problem is the key to a lasting solution."

# Purpose

The Five Whys method identifies the root cause of a problem by following the cause-effect chain through five iterations of asking "Why?"

# Description

Taiichi Ohno, the mastermind behind the Toyota Production System (TPS), described the Five Whys method as effective and straightforward: just ask five times "Why?".

The method is widespread and part of Kaizen, lean manufacturing, and Six Sigma.

Example: A problem was that fire was detected too late.
1. Why? The local sensor network did not work.
2. Why? An antenna in the forest was broken.
3. Why? The attachment of the antenna at the pole was broken.
4. Why? The antenna attachment was too old.
5. Why? The antenna was not maintained according to the required service schedule.

You can continue asking why questions if valuable. Five questions are generally sufficient to get to the real cause. You can also create several branches of the cause-effect chain for alternative causes.

The Five Whys method can be combined with the 6M method—a Five Whys cause-effect branch can be one entry in one of the 6M categories.
